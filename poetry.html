01
Poetry Pokhara

Where the Sky Breathes

Where the sky breathes and the soul listens —

staying open to all that life and nature offer,

flowing with the rhythm of nature,

grounded in the mountains of Pokhara,

where every sunrise writes a new poem

on the still surface of Phewa Lake.

— Dammar Khadayat

02
Reflection Pokhara

Code & Compassion

The same hand that writes a loop

holds a student's doubt gently.

In every line of code, a line of care —

for the machine must be precise,

but the teacher must be kind.

Both demand patience. Both require practice.

— Dammar Khadayat

03
Poetry Pokhara

The Annapurna Dawn

The peaks catch fire before the world wakes,

orange bleeding into snow,

and for a moment the mountain

holds the sun like a secret

it has been keeping all night.

Then the light spills, and everything begins.

— Dammar Khadayat

04
Reflection Pokhara

On Building & Being Built

We build systems that outlive us,

teach students who surpass us,

write papers that outlast the semester.

But what stays longest

is the moment a student's eyes light up

and the code finally runs.

— Dammar Khadayat

05
Poetry Pokhara

The Lakeside Hour

At Phewa, the water holds the sky

so carefully, as if afraid

to spill a single cloud.

Fishermen push their boats through mist,

temple bells echo across the valley,

and the mountains keep their ancient watch.

This is the hour I write from —

when the world is both still and beginning.

— Dammar Khadayat

06
Reflection Pokhara

The Debugging Hour

There is a kind of prayer in debugging —

the patient return to what you thought was right,

the humility to read each line again.

The error is not the enemy.

It is the teacher in disguise,

asking you to look more carefully

at the thing you almost understood.

— Dammar Khadayat

Poetry is the language of the soul. Technology is the language of possibility. Between them, I find my balance.

— Dammar Khadayat, Pokhara

About My Writing

I write because it keeps me honest. Code can be precise; teaching requires empathy; research demands rigour. Poetry lives in the space between all three — where the rhythm of a line matters as much as its meaning.

Most of these poems come from Pokhara, from early mornings at Phewa Lake or late evenings watching the Annapurna range catch the last light. The mountains here teach patience. The lake teaches stillness. Both inform how I approach design, code and the classroom.

My reflections on technology, education and mindful living are also published on LinkedIn and shared through workshops and guest sessions across Nepal.
